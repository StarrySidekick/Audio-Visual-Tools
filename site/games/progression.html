Progression Detective

Roman numerals and function

Songwriters name chords by their position in the key, so one progression works in every key. In C major, C is I, F is IV, G is V and A minor is vi. Uppercase is a major chord, lowercase is minor, and ° is diminished.

Each chord has a function. Tonic chords (I, vi, iii) feel like home. Subdominant chords (IV, ii) move away. Dominant chords (V, vii°) build tension that pulls back to I. Most progressions cycle tonic, subdominant, dominant, tonic.

Listening tips: follow the bass note, it plays each chord's root. V sounds like it is leaning forward; IV sounds like it is opening up. Minor chords (ii, iii, vi) sound softer or sadder than their neighbors. Borrowed chords (level 4) come from the minor key, so they sound suddenly darker or more epic.

Keys: 1–9 pick a numeral, Backspace undoes, Space replays.

Chord Roulette

Roll random chords and hear them back to back. Lock the ones you like, re-roll the rest, export to your DAW.

Settings
Chord types

Click a card to hear it alone. R rolls, Space plays or stops, 1–6 play one chord.

Why this is useful

Random chords break habits. Most people write the same few progressions because their hands go to the same shapes. Rolling forces you to hear combinations you would not choose, and locking lets you keep a surprising pair and search for what comes next.

Stay in one key only uses the seven chords built from that key's scale, so everything sounds related. Borrowed chords add chords from the parallel minor (♭VII, iv, ♭VI, ♭III), the usual source of an unexpected emotional turn. Anything goes picks any root and any chord type you allow; expect some beautiful accidents and some real clunkers.

Smooth voicing moves each note of a chord to the nearest note of the next one, the way a pianist would. Root position stacks every chord from its root, which sounds blockier but makes each chord's identity obvious. Export MIDI gives you one bar per chord at 90 BPM, ready to drag into Ableton.
